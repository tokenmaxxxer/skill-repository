# Five-whys recurring-pattern decision rules

Axis: five-whys-scope. 5 Whys fits simple-to-moderate problems; complex,
multi-cause problems need Kepner-Tregoe-style structured analysis instead
of a forced single chain. Source: https://blog.invgate.com/4-problem-management-root-cause-analysis-techniques-explained
and https://kepner-tregoe.com/blogs/how-5-whys-and-fishbone-diagrams-relate-to-kt-problem-analysis/

## Rules

- When a repeat/recurring inbound pattern is flagged, run five distinct
  question-shaped "why" lines before recording any hand-off decision —
  a hand-off with no preceding five-whys chain skips the diagnostic step
  the technique exists to force. Source: https://kepner-tregoe.com/blogs/beyond-5-whys-problem-solving-skills-for-real-life/
- When the five-whys answers converge on one causal chain (each "why"
  narrows the prior answer rather than branching), keep the finding
  in-role and resolve it as a KCS article rather than escalating —
  5 Whys is documented as fitting "simple to moderately difficult"
  problems, and a converged chain is exactly that shape. Source:
  https://blog.invgate.com/4-problem-management-root-cause-analysis-techniques-explained
- When the five-whys answers branch into multiple unrelated causes
  instead of one chain, route to product-discovery on that basis alone
  rather than forcing a single narrative — 5 Whys "tends to limit the
  analysis to just one avenue... whereas the problem might have many
  root causes," per Kepner-Tregoe's own comparison of the two
  techniques. Source: https://kepner-tregoe.com/blogs/how-5-whys-and-fishbone-diagrams-relate-to-kt-problem-analysis/
- Under a branching five-whys result, cite the Kepner-Tregoe follow-up
  question ("do we need to know why to take effective, meaningful
  action?") in the hand-off note rather than silently dropping the
  unresolved branches — this keeps the routing decision itself
  falsifiable by a later reader. Source: https://kepner-tregoe.com/blogs/beyond-5-whys-problem-solving-skills-for-real-life/
- **REMOVAL**: when a recurring-pattern entry already carries a
  convergent five-whys chain, omit a second independent root-cause
  technique (e.g. a full fishbone diagram) on the same entry — for a
  single-operator support desk this is over-instrumentation for a
  problem class the simpler technique already fits, per the same
  Kepner-Tregoe comparison. Source: https://blog.invgate.com/4-problem-management-root-cause-analysis-techniques-explained
