# Team Psychological Safety Measurement

## First: does this even need the procedure?

Check these before administering or interpreting anything, because forcing the full procedure where it doesn't fit wastes the user's time and can misapply a team-level construct to the wrong unit:

- **Is the goal to MEASURE, not to improve?** If the user wants "how do we make our team feel safer" or "what should we do to raise psychological safety," say plainly that this skill stops at measurement and interpretation — the evidence base for this construct is correlational (see Evidence grade below), so it cannot license an intervention prescription. Route any desired change to `hypothesis-testing` as a pre-registered experiment the team runs and evaluates itself.
- **Is the unit a team, not an individual?** Psychological safety is a team-level shared belief in Edmondson's (1999) formulation — it is measured via individual responses but interpreted as a property of the team, not of any one person. If the user wants to assess one person's individual comfort speaking up, say the construct doesn't apply as designed and don't force it.
- **Is there a real team of respondents who can answer confidentially?** If there's no team to survey — just a hypothetical, or a request to "guess" how safe a team feels without data — say this skill needs actual respondent data to measure anything, and offer to help design the administration instead of fabricating a reading.
- **Is this just a request to explain the concept?** If the user wants to understand what psychological safety is, with no survey to run or data to interpret, just answer directly — the full gated procedure below is for actually measuring or interpreting a real team's results.

Everything below applies when there's a real team, real respondents (existing or about to be surveyed), and the task is measurement or interpretation — not a change program.

## Evidence grade — read before citing this to anyone

- The instrument's structure and reliability reporting are **confirmed from the primary literature** (Edmondson 1999, ASQ; a 2025 Swedish elite-sport validation).
- Frazier et al. (2017, *Personnel Psychology*) meta-analyzed **136 independent samples and 22,000+ individuals**. But it is **correlational**: it aggregates self-report studies, carries documented publication-bias risk, and shows inflation of effect sizes where performance was measured subjectively. Edmondson's own founding study — a cross-sectional field study of 51 teams in one manufacturing company — disclaims causality for the design and calls her antecedent findings tentative given the 51-team sample, which she notes is small for multivariate analysis.
- **Korean-language validation of this scale was never examined in this research.** That is a coverage gap — nobody looked — not evidence that no validated Korean version exists. State it that way; never claim a validated Korean version does or does not exist.
- **The verbatim seven items were not verified in this research.** That is why this skill sources item wording from the original appendix rather than reproducing it from memory or a vendor page — a paraphrase would be a different instrument with unknown psychometric properties.
- What this skill delivers: **a correctly administered, correctly scored, honestly reported measurement** — not a prescription, not a benchmark verdict, not a causal claim.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Scope gate

Confirm: the goal is to measure team psychological safety (a diagnostic reading), and there is a real team of respondents who can answer confidentially.

- **Gate:** if the goal is actually "how do we improve psychological safety," stop here, say this skill measures and cannot prescribe, and point to `hypothesis-testing`. If the unit is an individual rather than a team, say the construct doesn't apply as designed and stop.

### Step 2 — Instrument sourcing gate

The seven items must be taken verbatim from the Edmondson (1999) ASQ appendix — not from memory, not from a vendor page (e.g. NovoPsych, which distributes a commercial sport-context redistribution with no reliability coefficients attached), not from a blog reproduction.

- **Gate:** name the source of the item wording in writing (the original ASQ appendix). If the original appendix is not accessible right now, stop and say so rather than administering paraphrased items — a paraphrase is a different instrument with unknown properties, and that fact must be surfaced, not silently worked around.

### Step 3 — Administration format gate

Use the 7-point scale with the ORIGINAL anchors: 1 = "very inaccurate" to 7 = "very accurate" — not agree/disagree. Confidentiality of individual responses is required.

- **Gate:** record which anchors were actually used and confirm they match the instrument being cited. If a vendor variant with agree/disagree anchors is deliberately used instead, record that choice explicitly as a deviation, and do not borrow the original's reliability coefficient for it — a different anchor format is a different instrument's psychometrics.

### Step 4 — Scoring gate

Reverse-score items 1, 3, and 5 (transform: `8 − raw` for each of those three items on this 7-point scale). Total score range is 7–49.

**Item numbers refer to the Edmondson (1999) appendix order.** Under any recorded variant (Step 3), identify the reverse-scored items **by wording, not by position** — a variant may reorder them, and reversing the wrong three items produces a total that passes every other gate while being silently wrong. If the variant's item ordering cannot be mapped back to the appendix, stop.

- **Gate:** the reverse-scoring step must be explicitly shown as done, with the transform stated per item, and the mapping to the appendix items named. An unreversed total — or one reversed by position under an unmapped variant — is invalid, not merely imprecise, and must not be reported as a valid score.

### Step 5 — Aggregation gate

Psychological safety is a team-level shared belief: aggregate individual responses to the team level, and report the team's **distribution**, not just a mean, along with respondent count and response rate.

- **Gate:** n and response rate must be stated. A team-level mean reported without its dispersion fails this gate — a team split between very-safe and very-unsafe respondents and a team that is uniformly middling can share the same mean while meaning opposite things, and only the distribution reveals which one this team is.

### Step 6 — Reliability reporting gate

Report the internal-consistency coefficient for the context actually being cited, naming its source:

| Context | Coefficient | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Original manufacturing-company study | α = .82 | Edmondson (1999) |
| 2025 Swedish elite-sport validation, one-factor solution | ω = 0.72 | Swedish elite-sport validation (2025) |
| An independent validation study | α = .95 | **Unverified — the specific study was not sourced in this research. The coefficient appears in secondary literature citing an independent validation whose full bibliographic reference was not recovered. Do not cite this coefficient without sourcing the study by name.** |

- **Gate:** no single number is presented as "the" reliability of the instrument in general. The coefficient cited must name the study it came from, matched to the context of the administration being reported.

### Step 7 — Interpretation gate (the hard boundary)

Report the measurement and its uncertainty. Do not attach an intervention plan, a benchmark verdict ("this is a healthy team"), or a causal story. No cutoff or norm bands were verified in this research — do not invent "good" or "bad" score bands, and do not claim none exist.

- **Gate:** the final report contains no prescription and no norm comparison. If the user asks for change recommendations anyway, the report states explicitly that this evidence base — correlational, meta-analyzed but carrying self-report publication-bias risk — does not license intervention claims, and routes any desired change to `hypothesis-testing` as a pre-registered experiment the team runs and evaluates itself.

## What must never appear in output

- Verbatim item text reproduced from memory or a vendor page (not verified here — source it from the Edmondson 1999 ASQ appendix instead).
- Agree/disagree anchors presented as the original scale's anchors.
- A single canonical reliability number presented as "the" reliability of the instrument.
- Any claim that a named intervention raises psychological safety.
- Any claim that a validated Korean version does or does not exist (state the coverage gap instead).
- Invented statistics, norms, or cutoff/benchmark score bands — none were verified.
