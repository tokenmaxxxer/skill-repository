# Canvas-construction decision rules

Decision rules for choosing which canvas shape to fill (BMC vs. Lean
Canvas), the block fill order within the chosen shape, and named
construction failure modes to check for. Research trail: layer 1
(Strategyzer/Osterwalder primary sourcing, Ash Maurya/LeanStack primary
sourcing).

## Trigger

Apply this skill when choosing between the Business Model Canvas (BMC)
and Lean Canvas for a business at a given stage, when filling a
canvas's blocks and deciding fill order, or when checking a filled
canvas against a known construction failure mode.

## Procedure

1. When the business is pre-product-market-fit, choose Lean Canvas;
   when it is an already-running business being fine-tuned, choose BMC
   (rule 1).
2. When filling BMC, group its 9 blocks into
   Desirability/Feasibility/Viability and fill in that order, deriving
   Cost Structure last (rule 2).
3. When filling Lean Canvas, fill riskiest-assumption-last: Problem +
   Customer Segments first, Unfair Advantage last (rule 3).
4. Before writing Solution on a Lean Canvas, validate Problem +
   Customer via a Problem Interview (rule 4).
5. When reviewing a filled canvas, check it against the
   "Innovator's Bias" and "living hypothesis, not static plan" failure
   modes (rule 5).
6. When a canvas's blocks stop changing after real customer evidence
   accumulates, flag it as a stalled hypothesis document rather than a
   validated one (rule 6).

## Output shape

A named canvas choice (BMC or Lean Canvas) with the stage-based reason,
blocks filled in the sequence this skill specifies, and an explicit
check against the named failure modes before the canvas is treated as
done.

## Rules

1. When the business is a brand-new, pre-product-market-fit venture,
   choose Lean Canvas; when it is an already-running business being
   fine-tuned, choose the Business Model Canvas — Lean Canvas swaps
   BMC's four "running-a-business" blocks (Key Partners, Key
   Activities, Key Resources, Customer Relationships) for four
   "starting-a-business" blocks (Problem, Solution, Key Metrics,
   Unfair Advantage), reframing the question from "how do I run this"
   to "is there a real business here."
   source: https://medium.com/lean-stack/why-lean-canvas-vs-business-model-canvas-af62c0f250f0

2. When filling the Business Model Canvas, group its 9 blocks into
   Desirability (Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels,
   Customer Relationships), Feasibility (Key Resources, Key
   Activities, Key Partnerships), and Viability (Revenue Streams, Cost
   Structure), and fill customer-first, cost/revenue last — Cost
   Structure is a derived block, computed only after Key
   Resources/Activities/Partnerships are defined.
   source: https://www.strategyzer.com/business-models-the-toolkit-to-design-a-disruptive-company

3. When filling a Lean Canvas, follow the riskiest-assumption-last fill
   order: Problem + Customer Segments together first (a problem only
   exists in the context of a specific customer), then UVP, then
   Solution, then Channels, then Revenue + Cost together, then Key
   Metrics, then Unfair Advantage last.
   source: https://medium.com/lean-stack/what-is-the-right-fill-order-for-a-lean-canvas-f8071d0c6c8c

4. Before writing the Solution block on a Lean Canvas, validate Problem
   + Customer Segments via a Problem Interview that extracts problems
   indirectly (get the full story of the interviewee's last relevant
   experience) rather than asking leading, jargon-laden questions —
   the interview script itself instructs interviewers to avoid the
   word "problem."
   source: https://blog.leanstack.com/the-updated-problem-interview-script-and-a-new-canvas-1e43ff267a5d

5. When reviewing a filled canvas (BMC or Lean Canvas), check it
   against "Innovator's Bias" — falling in love with the first
   solution before the problem is validated — and treat the canvas as
   a living hypothesis document to be revised against evidence, not a
   static one-time plan.
   source: https://medium.com/lean-stack/love-the-problem-not-your-solution-65cfbfb1916b

6. **[removal]** When a canvas's blocks have stopped changing despite
   real customer evidence accumulating (interviews, usage data,
   Problem Interview findings), do not treat the canvas as "done" —
   flag it as a stalled hypothesis document; a canvas that never
   updates against evidence has stopped functioning as the living
   document Lean Canvas practice requires, even if it was filled
   correctly at the start.
   source: https://medium.com/lean-stack/love-the-problem-not-your-solution-65cfbfb1916b
