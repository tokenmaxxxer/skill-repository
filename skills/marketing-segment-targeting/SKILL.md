# Segment selection and targeting

Research trail: the STP (Segmentation-Targeting-Positioning) framework
survey via Umbrex's marketing-frameworks reference and CMU Swartz
Center's STP teaching deck, and Adience's B2B-vs-B2C marketer guide, all
fetched this session.

## Trigger

Apply this skill when evaluating or ranking candidate segments,
defining a B2B vs. B2C target segment, or reviewing whether a
previously-selected segment should stay in the active target list.

## Procedure

1. When evaluating candidate segments, choose only ones that clear
   measurable, substantial, accessible, and actionable together
   (rule 1).
2. When ranking segments for prioritization, weigh size/growth,
   profitability/CLV, competitive intensity, access, and capability/
   brand fit together rather than size alone (rule 2).
3. For a B2B target market, define the segment by firmographics plus
   buying-persona job role rather than consumer demographics alone
   (rule 3).
4. When a segment's competitive intensity is high and the UVP from
   [[positioning-differentiation]] rule 2 is weak there, deprioritize
   that segment in favor of one where the UVP is harder to match
   (rule 4).
5. When a segment fails the actionable test, drop it from the active
   target list rather than keeping it for future reference (rule 5).
6. When a previously-selected segment's growth or profitability signal
   degrades over successive review cycles, cut it and redirect its
   budget to a segment currently clearing the CLV/competitive-intensity
   bar (rule 6).

## Output shape

A targeting decision: the applicable rule number(s), the segment(s)
evaluated, and the prioritize/deprioritize/drop verdict.

## Rules

1. When evaluating candidate segments, choose only ones that clear
   measurable, substantial, accessible, and actionable together — reject
   a segment that fails even one, since an unreachable or unmeasurable
   segment cannot be targeted regardless of its appeal. source:
   https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning/

2. When ranking segments for prioritization, weigh segment size/growth,
   profitability or CLV, competitive intensity, access, and fit with
   capability/brand together rather than picking the largest segment by
   size alone, since size alone ignores whether you can profitably win
   it. source: https://www.cmu.edu/swartz-center-for-entrepreneurship/assets/connects-fall-2024/stp-fall-2024-connect-v2.pdf

3. For a B2B target market, define the segment by firmographics
   (company size/industry) plus the buying persona's job role rather
   than by consumer demographics alone, since a B2B purchase decision is
   made by a role inside an org, not by an individual consumer profile.
   source: https://www.adience.com/blog/insights/what-marketers-need-to-know-b2b-vs-b2c/

4. When a segment's competitive intensity is high and the UVP from
   [[positioning-differentiation]] rule 2 is weak there, deprioritize
   that segment in favor of one where the same UVP is harder to match,
   rather than contesting the crowded segment on message strength alone
   — evaluation lenses for targeting explicitly include competitive
   intensity alongside size and fit. source:
   https://www.cmu.edu/swartz-center-for-entrepreneurship/assets/connects-fall-2024/stp-fall-2024-connect-v2.pdf

5. **REMOVAL**: When a segment fails the actionable test (no channel,
   message, or offer can reach it distinctly), drop it from the active
   target list rather than keeping it "for future reference" — an
   unreachable segment consumes positioning and channel-planning
   attention without being actionable now. source:
   https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning/

6. When a previously-selected segment's growth or profitability signal
   degrades over successive review cycles, cut it from the active
   target list and redirect its budget to a segment currently clearing
   the CLV/competitive-intensity bar, instead of continuing to fund a
   segment on the strength of its original selection rationale alone —
   targeting selection criteria are size, growth potential, and
   strategic fit, re-evaluated, not a one-time verdict. source:
   https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/marketing-frameworks/stp-framework-segmentation-targeting-positioning/
