# User Discovery — objective criteria and their sources

Evidence grades: ●●● peer-reviewed/quantitative · ●●○ industry standard · ●○○ practitioner folklore (use, but know it's soft).

## Stopping rule — saturation (●●●)

Guest, Bunce & Johnson (2006), 60-interview corpus: ~80% of all codes appeared within the first 6 interviews; code saturation by interview 12 — for a *narrow question in a homogeneous segment*. Hennink et al. (2017): code saturation 9-17, but *meaning* saturation (no new interpretive insight) 16-24. PLOS One (2020) operational formula: (new themes in a run of 2-3 interviews ÷ themes in the base set) — stop at ≤5% (≈6 interviews typical) for light work, 0% (≈11-14) for thorough. Heterogeneous samples need more. Practical rule: 2-3 consecutive interviews with nothing new = saturated for that segment.

## Question discipline — Mom Test (●●○) + hypothetical bias (●●●)

Fitzpatrick's three rules: talk about their life not your idea; ask about specific past events not future hypotheticals; talk less. The quantitative backbone: stated WTP overstates real WTP by ~21% on average (2019 meta-analysis, JAMS), worse for specialty/emotional categories — so any "would you buy/use" answer is inflated by construction. An interview that produced only compliments and predictions is void — no evidence collected.

## Evidence hierarchy (●●○, converging)

Observed behavior and actual spend > specific recounted past events > opinions/predictions. Grounded in self-report bias literature (social desirability, recall distortion — ●●●) and independently adopted by contextual inquiry (Beyer & Holtzblatt), Mom Test, and Torres's "ladder of evidence" (support tickets/reviews are plentiful but weak; story-based interviews are strong). No researched numeric threshold exists for "N mentions = pattern" (●○○ gap) — report prevalence and unprompted-vs-prompted instead of declaring significance.

## Contextual inquiry (●●○)

Beyer & Holtzblatt's four principles: context (observe in the real environment), partnership, interpretation (verify your reading with the participant in the moment), focus. Rationale: retrospective accounts drop the interruptions, workarounds, and tacit steps that design actually hinges on; asking "why" at the moment of action captures them. Cost: hours per participant; low scalability. No controlled trial proves superiority over interviews — the case rests on the (●●●) recall-bias literature.

## Recruiting & incentives

Screeners: recruit on behavior, hide qualifying answers among distractors, put the biggest excluder first (●○○ practice, NN/g). Professional respondents: nonprobability panelists answered a median 10 surveys/30 days vs 3 for probability panels, 2-3× more multi-panel membership; correlates with straight-lining and speeding (●●● NORC). Incentives: 46-RCT meta-analysis (n=109,648) — cash RR 1.25, vouchers 1.19, lottery 1.12 for response; *prepaid beats conditional* consistently; data quality largely unaffected but sample composition can skew (●●●).

## Analysis rigor

Thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke 2006, ●●○): familiarize → code → candidate themes → **check themes against the whole dataset** → define/name → write. The check-back is the bias control. Affinity/KJ method (●●○): bottom-up silent sorting, no preconceived categories. Intercoder reliability: Cohen's kappa with Landis & Koch bands (0.61-0.80 substantial, >0.81 almost perfect) — but the bands are admittedly arbitrary and kappa distorts under uneven code prevalence (●○○ as a hard threshold; use as a signal). Qualitative trustworthiness (Lincoln & Guba, ●●○): credibility (triangulation, member checking), transferability (thick description), dependability, confirmability (audit trail) — the qualitative counterpart to validity/reliability, and the right vocabulary when someone demands "statistical significance" from interview research.

## Personas (●○○)

No controlled evidence that personas improve design outcomes; the research gap is itself the finding. Defensible version: data-driven persona = cluster of observed behavioral patterns with evidence attached, dated, and refreshed. Aspirational/demographic personas without research are fiction — say so.

## Diary studies (●○○ method / ●●● underlying bias case)

Fit: longitudinal, habitual, or low-frequency behavior where single-session recall fails. Log at the moment of the event; follow up while memory is fresh. Duration practice: ~1 week for high-frequency, 2-3 weeks for lower. Dropout grows with duration.

## Boundary: discovery ≠ demand proof

Discovery finds and shapes problems; it cannot prove purchase intent or market size. Graduating steps: quantitative survey with real sampling (market-recon criteria), or incentive-compatible demand test (pre-sale, paid pilot). Never let "9 of 10 interviewees loved it" stand in for demand.
