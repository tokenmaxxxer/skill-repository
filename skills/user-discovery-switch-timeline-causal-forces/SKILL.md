# Switch-timeline reconstruction: JTBD four-forces causal framing

Research trail: Bob Moesta & Chris Spiek's Jobs-to-be-Done four-forces model (jobstobedone.org), koji.so 2026 JTBD interview guide on switch-interview protocol structure. All searched this session.

## Rules

1. When a hypothesis claims a customer will adopt a new solution, walk them back through the actual story of their last switch (or their last decision not to switch) rather than asking about future adoption intent — the switch interview reconstructs push/pull/anxiety/habit in the customer's own words from a real event, which is strictly more falsifiable than a forward-looking intent question. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

2. When reconstructing a switch story, explicitly ask what was wrong with the old way (push) before asking what attracted them to the new way (pull) — push and pull are separate forces with separate evidence, and a script that only asks about the destination misses the origin-side pain that is often the actual pain-confirmation evidence. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

3. When a customer describes switching, ask what almost stopped them or what they were unsure about (anxiety) — a switch story with no anxiety captured is incomplete, because Push+Pull>Anxiety+Habit means anxiety is half of what determines whether the switch happened at all, not a footnote to it. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

4. When a customer describes NOT switching despite naming a real problem, probe specifically for habit (what about the old way they were comfortable with or had built workflow around) — habit inertia can outweigh a confirmed pain, so a pain-confirmed verdict without a habit check risks over-predicting willingness to actually change behavior. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

5. When budgeting interview time for a switch-timeline reconstruction, reserve roughly the first third of the interview for warm-up plus timeline reconstruction before moving to any other hypothesis line — a rushed timeline reconstruction collapses push/pull/anxiety/habit into a single vague narrative instead of four separately checkable data points. source: https://www.koji.so/blog/jobs-to-be-done-interview-guide-2026

6. When a stated pain has no corresponding push-side switch story (the customer names the problem but has never taken any action because of it), tag the pain as unconfirmed-by-behavior rather than counting it toward a pain-confirmed verdict — a switch story with zero push evidence is the behavioral falsification case for a hypothesis, distinct from just not having asked yet. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

7. When two interviewees report the same push+pull pattern but opposite anxiety/habit outcomes (one switched, one didn't), record both stories rather than only the one that confirms the hypothesis — a verdict that only samples switch-completers systematically overweights pull and underweights the anxiety/habit resistance that a real product will also face. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

8. **REMOVAL**: When a script has separate questions for "why did you switch" and "why do you like the new product," drop the second as its own question — pull toward the new solution is already captured inside the switch-timeline reconstruction (rule 2), and a standalone post-hoc "why do you like it" question invites rationalized opinion rather than reusing the causal timeline already established. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/

9. **REMOVAL**: When an interview guide asks "on a scale of 1-10 how much anxiety did you feel switching," drop the numeric-anxiety-scale question — anxiety is captured through the concrete story of hesitation/doubt named in the switch timeline (rule 3), and a bolted-on numeric scale reintroduces the opinion-question fiction the past-behavior axis (question-design-past-behavior rule 8) already rules out. source: https://jobstobedone.org/the-four-forces/
