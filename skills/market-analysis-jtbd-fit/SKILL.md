# JTBD-landscape verdict rules

Decision rules for naming the customer job and judging whether this
spec's differentiation holds against the strongest competing
alternative (this rulebook's `produces.JTBD-landscape verdict` field).
Research trail: layer 2 (Christensen JTBD, Ulwick Outcome-Driven
Innovation, verified at source) plus layer 1 (practitioner job-framing
practice).

## Trigger

Apply this skill when naming the customer job a spec competes for,
identifying the strongest competing alternative, drafting a job or
outcome statement, or writing the differentiation verdict against that
alternative.

## Procedure

1. When naming the job, phrase it at the level of underlying progress,
   not product category, using the circumstance-plus-motivation
   structured form (rules 1, 4).
2. When identifying the strongest competing alternative, ask what the
   customer would do instead if this product did not exist, including
   "do nothing," rather than defaulting to the nearest same-category
   competitor (rule 2).
3. When the job has a measurable outcome, state the differentiation
   verdict directly against that outcome metric rather than as an
   unquantified feature comparison, and require any "better" verdict to
   name the specific outcome and evidence (rules 3, 5).
4. When the differentiation is actually about price rather than job
   performance, state it as "price-differentiated" rather than folding
   it into a "wins the job" verdict (rule 6).
5. When multiple candidate jobs are listed, cut to the one the
   strongest evidence supports as primary before finalizing the verdict
   (rule 7).
6. When the competing alternative is "doing nothing," address why now
   in the verdict rather than only comparing features against a
   nonexistent baseline (rule 8).
7. When the job statement and the competitor-mapping/five-forces
   sections name different competitors, reconcile them explicitly
   before publishing (rule 9).
8. When collecting outcome statements from interviews, phrase each in
   Ulwick's "minimize/increase the <metric> when <context>" syntax
   rather than as a restated feature request (rule 10).

## Output shape

A job statement in circumstance-plus-motivation form, a named strongest
competing alternative, and a differentiation verdict stated against a
measurable outcome (or explicitly price-differentiated), reconciled
against the competitor-mapping/five-forces sections.

## Rules

1. When naming the job, phrase it at the level of the underlying
   progress the customer is trying to make ("get a quick, filling
   commute snack"), not at the level of the product category ("buy a
   milkshake") — job statements framed at product-category level hide
   the real competing alternatives, per Christensen's own milkshake
   case. source: https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/jobs-to-be-done-framework
2. When identifying the strongest competing alternative, do not default
   to the nearest same-category competitor — ask what the customer would
   do instead if this product did not exist at all (including "do
   nothing" or a manual workaround); the true competing alternative is
   whatever currently gets the job done, regardless of category.
   source: https://jobs-to-be-done.com/what-is-jobs-to-be-done-fea59c8e39eb
3. When the job has a measurable, quantitative outcome the customer
   uses to judge success (e.g. "minimize time from request to
   approval"), state the differentiation verdict against that outcome
   metric directly (faster/slower, cheaper/costlier, by how much) rather
   than as an unquantified feature comparison — Ulwick's Outcome-Driven
   Innovation treats desired outcomes as the stable, measurable unit of
   comparison. source: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/
4. When drafting the job statement, use the structured
   circumstance-plus-motivation form ("when I <circumstance>, I want to
   <motivation>, so I can <expected outcome>") rather than a bare noun
   phrase — the structured form forces the circumstance and outcome to
   be stated, which a noun-phrase job name silently omits.
   source: https://analyticsengines.com/resources/insights/outcome-driven-innovation-pt-2-implementing-a-job-to-be-done-framework/
5. When the differentiation verdict claims "better," require it to name
   the specific desired outcome it is better on and by what evidence —
   an unqualified "better" verdict fails the same shape test evidence-
   rigor requires elsewhere in this rulebook: a claim with no cited
   source or metric does not count as a verdict. source:
   https://researcher.life/blog/article/primary-vs-secondary-sources-differences-and-examples/
6. When the spec's differentiation is not actually about performing the
   job better but about a lower price for the same job performance,
   state the verdict as "price-differentiated, not outcome-
   differentiated" rather than folding it into the same "wins the job"
   language used for outcome-based advantages — the two are different
   defensibility profiles and collapsing them hides that a
   price-only edge erodes faster. source:
   https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/
7. **REMOVAL**: when the proposal lists multiple candidate "jobs" the
   spec could compete on, do not carry all of them into the record —
   cut every job statement except the one the strongest evidence (user
   interviews, usage data, or the product-discovery record if one
   exists) supports as primary; a multi-job verdict section reads as
   hedging, not analysis. source:
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03380-y
8. When a competing alternative is "doing nothing" (the customer
   currently has no solution and tolerates the problem), the
   differentiation verdict must address why now — what changed to make
   inaction no longer the winning alternative — rather than only
   comparing features against a nonexistent baseline. source:
   https://jobs-to-be-done.com/what-is-jobs-to-be-done-fea59c8e39eb
9. When the job statement and the five-forces/competitor-mapping
   sections name different "competitors," reconcile them explicitly
   before publishing — the JTBD analysis often surfaces a competing
   alternative the category-based competitor list missed (the
   milkshake-vs-bananas case), and an unreconciled mismatch signals one
   of the two sections used the wrong frame. source:
   https://online.hbs.edu/blog/post/jobs-to-be-done-examples
10. When outcome statements are being collected from interviews, phrase
    each as "minimize/increase the <metric> when <context>" (Ulwick's
    outcome-statement syntax) rather than as a restated feature request
    — feature requests describe a solution the customer imagined, not
    the outcome the differentiation verdict should actually be judged
    against. source: https://strategyn.com/jobs-to-be-done-template/
