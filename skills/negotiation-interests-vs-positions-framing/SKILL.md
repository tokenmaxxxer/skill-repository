# Interests-vs-positions framing

Principled-negotiation rules for reframing a stalled or irreconcilable
positional exchange around each side's underlying interests, rather
than trading further concessions on the stated position.

## Trigger

Use when a negotiation has stalled into repeated positional concessions
with no underlying interest identified, when drafting an opening
proposal that states a position without the interest behind it, or when
a counterpart's stated position seems irreconcilable with your own and
neither side has yet asked what interest the position protects.

## Procedure

1. Before stating a position, identify and write down the underlying
   interest it is meant to protect; treat a position drafted with no
   stated interest as incomplete preparation, not a finished opening
   offer (rule 1).
2. When a negotiation stalls on irreconcilable positions, stop trading
   further concessions on the position itself and instead surface each
   side's underlying interests to search for options that satisfy both
   (rule 2).

## Output shape

A documented interest behind each stated position, and/or a reframed
set of options that satisfy the surfaced interests instead of the
stalled positions.

## Decision rules

### 1. Identify the underlying interest before stating a position
- **Condition**: drafting an opening proposal, or any point at which a position is about to be stated to a counterpart
- **Choice**: before stating the position, identify and write down the underlying interest it is meant to protect (the need, concern, or goal behind the number or demand); treat a proposal drafted around a position with no stated interest as incomplete preparation, not a finished opening offer
- **Why**: positions are what a party says it wants; interests are what actually motivates that want. Arguing over positions produces unwise agreements and strains the relationship, while surfacing interests opens room for options neither side had considered
- **Source**: Fisher, Ury & Patton (Harvard Negotiation Project), summarized at Beyond Intractability, "Summary of Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In", https://www.beyondintractability.org/bksum/fisher-getting
- **Counter-example test**: an opening proposal that states "we need exclusivity for 24 months" with no written record of the interest that duration is meant to protect (e.g. recouping a specific integration cost) fails this rule.

### 2. Reframe a stalled positional exchange around interests instead of trading another concession
- **Condition**: a negotiation has stalled into repeated back-and-forth concessions with each side's position seeming irreconcilable, and neither side has yet asked what interest the position protects
- **Choice**: stop trading further concessions on the position itself; instead, surface each side's underlying interests and search for options that satisfy both, rather than splitting the difference on the stated positions
- **Why**: positions that look irreconcilable can rest on interests that are not in conflict at all — the Camp David pattern, where Israel's and Egypt's positions on Sinai territory were irreconcilable but the interests behind them (security, sovereignty) were not, and were satisfied by demilitarization instead of a territorial split
- **Source**: Beyond Intractability, "Summary of Getting to Yes: Negotiating Agreement Without Giving In", https://www.beyondintractability.org/bksum/fisher-getting
- **Counter-example test**: a negotiation stuck on a fifth round of concessions on a single number, with no discussion of what interest either side's number is meant to serve, fails this rule — repeating the concession pattern is not a reframe.
