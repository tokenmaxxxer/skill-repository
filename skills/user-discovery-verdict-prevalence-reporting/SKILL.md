# Verdict reporting: prevalence, prompted/unprompted, contradictions

Research trail: qualitative saturation-reporting norms (tandfonline 2025 review), Mom Test evidence-quality distinctions, JTBD switch-story evidence asymmetry (rules cross-referenced from switch-timeline-causal-forces). All searched this session.

## Rules

1. When issuing a pain-confirmed or not-confirmed verdict, state prevalence as "N of M" (interviews showing the pain over total interviews run), never as a percentage alone or a qualitative descriptor ("most," "many") — a bare qualitative descriptor hides the actual sample size and cannot be checked against the saturation log's interview count. source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08911762.2025.2590757

2. When a theme surfaced without the interviewer having asked about it directly (the interviewee volunteered it), flag that occurrence as unprompted in the evidence log, distinct from a theme that only appeared after a direct question — unprompted mentions are stronger prevalence evidence than prompted ones because a direct question can itself suggest the answer, so conflating the two overstates how independently the pain was corroborated. source: https://www.ventureforall.com/p/from-assumptions-to-evidence-are

3. When computing the N in an "N of M" prevalence claim, count only `behavioral`-or-higher-tagged instances (per the evidence-strength-tagging axis) toward N, never `opinion`-tagged instances — a prevalence count that includes opinion-tier claims silently launders weak evidence into what reads as a hard count. source: https://www.ventureforall.com/p/from-assumptions-to-evidence-are

4. When at least one interview contradicts the majority pattern (reports not having the pain, or reports a switch story with no push evidence per the switch-timeline axis), name that contradicting evidence explicitly in the verdict rather than omitting it because the majority already confirms the hypothesis — a verdict that only cites confirming entries misrepresents the actual spread of evidence and hides a residual the next role (requirements-engineering) needs to know about. source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08911762.2025.2590757

5. When the unprompted mention count for a theme is zero (every mention only appeared after the interviewer asked directly), downgrade the verdict's confidence language accordingly (e.g. "suggested when asked" rather than "a recurring pain") — an entirely prompted theme is weaker prevalence evidence than one volunteered spontaneously, and the verdict wording should reflect that difference rather than stating both cases identically. source: https://www.ventureforall.com/p/from-assumptions-to-evidence-are

6. When M (total interviews) is below the saturation-appropriate range for the population (per the saturation-stopping-rule axis), state that explicitly in the verdict rather than presenting N of M as if saturation were reached — a prevalence fraction from an under-sampled round is a preliminary signal, not a confirmed verdict, and presenting it without that caveat overstates confidence. source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0277953621008558

7. When a pain-confirmed verdict is issued, cite the specific evidence-log entries it rests on (interview ID + entry) rather than asserting the prevalence fraction as a bare summary number — a verdict traceable to named log entries lets a reviewer re-derive the N-of-M count independently instead of trusting an unverifiable tally. source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08911762.2025.2590757

8. **REMOVAL**: When a verdict cites only the count of confirming interviews and omits the total M, drop that reporting shape — a bare "N confirmed" with no denominator is not a prevalence claim (rule 1) and must not be accepted as satisfying the N-of-M requirement, even if N itself is accurate. source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08911762.2025.2590757

9. **REMOVAL**: When a verdict pools prompted and unprompted mentions into one undifferentiated count to inflate apparent prevalence, drop that pooled-count practice — rule 2's prompted/unprompted distinction exists specifically so a verdict cannot claim a theme is "recurring" on the strength of mentions the interviewer's own question produced. source: https://www.ventureforall.com/p/from-assumptions-to-evidence-are
