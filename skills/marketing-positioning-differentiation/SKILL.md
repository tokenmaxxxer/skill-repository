# Positioning and differentiation

Research trail: Miro's 2026 product-positioning guide, Digital Applied's
2026 B2B competitive-positioning playbook, FasterCapital's UVP/
differentiation/positioning survey, and monday.com's 2026 positioning
guide, all fetched this session.

## Rules

1. When customers currently solve the problem with a competitor, a
   workaround, or by doing nothing, name that specific alternative in
   the positioning statement's "instead of" clause before drafting the
   value proposition, since you cannot state what makes you different
   without first naming what you are different from — B2B positioning
   research finds "do nothing" is the most common competitor and
   opportunities are lost to the status quo when it goes unnamed.
   source: https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/competitive-positioning-framework-2026-b2b-strategy-playbook

2. When a unique attribute is claimed, choose only attributes that are
   both meaningful to the buyer and difficult for a competitor to
   replicate quickly — an attribute a competitor can copy in a sprint
   does not earn a place in the positioning statement. source:
   https://fastercapital.com/topics/unique-value-proposition,-differentiation,-and-positioning.html/1

3. For an audience unfamiliar with the market category, pick a category
   frame that borrows an existing, already-understood mental model
   ("X for Y") rather than inventing a new category name, since
   positioning's building blocks (target audience, market category,
   UVP, proof points) require the category slot to already mean
   something to the reader. source:
   https://miro.com/product-development/product-positioning/

4. When proof points are drafted, keep only claims a buyer could
   independently verify (a number, a named customer, a third-party
   citation) and cut adjective-only claims ("best-in-class", "leading")
   that carry no verifiable referent, since unverifiable proof points
   do not raise credibility and dilute the ones that do. source:
   https://fastercapital.com/topics/unique-value-proposition,-differentiation,-and-positioning.html/1

5. **REMOVAL**: When a positioning statement lists more than one unique
   value proposition, cut it down to the single proposition that is
   most meaningful and least replicable, and move the rest to
   supporting proof points — a positioning statement with multiple
   simultaneous "unique" claims signals none of them is actually the
   differentiator. source: https://monday.com/blog/rnd/product-positioning/

6. When the target segment shifts (per [[segment-targeting]]), pick the
   "instead of" alternative for that segment specifically rather than
   reusing the prior segment's competitive frame, since the credible
   alternative a mid-market buyer compares against is rarely the same
   one an enterprise buyer compares against.
